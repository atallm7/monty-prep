## Flagged checks

### Charge-pump output resistance (q0284-q0289)

The pages use $R_{out}\approx\sqrt{R_{SSL}^2+R_{FSL}^2}$ with $R_{SSL}=1/(fC_{fly})$ and $R_{FSL}=8R_{sw}+4R_{ESR}$. I checked this three ways:

- **The two limits are correct.** They agree with the Seeman-Sanders charge-multiplier analysis: every element carries $a=1$, so $R_{SSL}=1/(fC)$ and $R_{FSL}=2\sum R_i a_i^2=8R_{sw}$. They also agree with the classic datasheet sum $2(R_{SW1}+R_{SW3}+ESR)+2(R_{SW2}+R_{SW4}+ESR)$.
- **The exact result for this doubler.** I derived the exact steady-state result for the two-phase doubler with equal switches: $R_{out}=\frac{1}{fC}\coth\!\left(\frac{1}{8fR_{sw}C}\right)$. A time-step simulation reproduces it to within 0.01%.
- **The root-sum-square is a good approximation.** It always overestimates the exact value, by at most 8.8% near the crossover. For the worked example it gives 8.25 Ω against 8.17 Ω exact, so it is slightly conservative. The linear sum (10 Ω here) overestimates by up to about 50%.

I added the exact expression and the "≤9% conservative" note to q0284 and q0288, and a pointer in q0289's assumptions.

### q0274: buck vs boost conduction loss

All the numbers check out: 91.3% vs 95.0% at equal 5 W, 97.2% for both synchronous designs, and 95.6% vs 91.3% at equal output current. The framing was misleading, though. The spoken answer said "the main reason is current", but at equal power and the same voltage pair both inductors carry the same 1.35 A, because the inductor always sits on the low-voltage side. In the page's own table the gap comes almost entirely from the diode, which conducts 74% of the period in the boost and 26% in the buck. I reworded the answer and the short version, and added a note under the table. The $I_{out}/(1-D)$ penalty is now described as applying per amp of load, or at high boost ratios.
