# QA review: batch b32 (q0554-q0573)

I recomputed every quiz answer (101 items) and every worked number with `node`. That covered copper resistance and drift, Faraday pickup, CMRR and SNR gains, Kelvin errors, ENOB, ground bounce, trapezoid spectra, the loop and cable radiation formulas against the FCC limits, plane-wave pickup, RC and bode corners, capacitive coupling with ramp edges, broadside capacitance, desense, skin depth, absorption and reflection (Ott's R and R_H), aperture SE, via-fence pitch and cavity modes. All quiz answers, indices and tolerances are correct.

I screenshotted every figure and sim in the app, and rendered every schematic with `preview-circuit`. That included q0559, q0561, q0563–q0565, q0568 and q0570–q0573, which had never been viewed. The schematics are electrically correct: Kelvin and CSA polarity, current arrows in the DM/CM figures, bidirectional TVS and the shield network. Sim params match catalog.json. The validator reports 0 errors.

**Standards check:** The FCC Part 15 Class B 3 m limits (40/43.5/46/54 dBµV/m) and the CISPR 32 Class B 10 m limits (30/37 dBµV/m) are correct. The conducted range of 150 kHz to 30 MHz and the IEC 61000-4-2/-3/-4/-5/-6 scopes are correct too. No clause numbers are invented.

**Most serious:**
- **q0561:** the figure caption said the 10 cm² loop crosses the FCC limit near 70 MHz. The plotted formula crosses at about 48 MHz; I also added the 3 cm² crossing (87 MHz) and corrected the 1 cm² one to about 185 MHz.
- **q0562:** the guidance said to keep plane slots "perpendicular to current flow". That is backwards: a slot across the return path is the worst case, so it should run along the current.
- **q0564:** "C–L–C gives 40 dB/decade". A pi filter is third order (up to 60 dB/decade); 40 dB/decade is a single L–C section.
- **q0555:** "about 10 nH/cm for a trace over a plane" is the isolated-wire value. Over a plane it is about 3–5 nH/cm.
- **Cable CM radiation formula:** q0559 and q0561 asserted that the 1.26×10⁻⁶ formula "includes ground reflection; free space is half". The factor of 2 depends on the textbook's convention (per-wire vs total current), so both are now hedged.
