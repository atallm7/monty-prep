## Most serious
- **q0520 mode-conversion factor of 2.** The page stated $V_{cm}/V_d=\tan(\pi f\Delta t)$. The page's own definitions give $V_{cm}/V_d=\tfrac12\tan$. The plain tan is the power-normalised mixed-mode ratio $|S_{cd21}/S_{dd21}|$.
  - So "20 ps at 5 GHz is about a third of the differential amplitude" was wrong. The common-mode voltage is about 16% of the differential swing; the S-parameter ratio is −10 dB.
  - Fixed the formula, plot title and caption, worked example, quiz wording and spoken answers.
- **q0528 (thick substrate):** I recomputed everything with the model.
  - The single-ended numbers are right: 1.517 mm on 0.8 mm gives 50.0 Ω (not 50.3); on 1.6 mm it gives 72.6 Ω (+45%), εeff goes from 3.22 to 3.06, and the repair width is 3.07 mm.
  - "+45%" holds only for 50 Ω traces (w/h ≈ 1.9). It is +35% at w/h = 1 and +18–27% for narrow traces. The page's claim of "still more than 30%" was wrong; hedges corrected.
  - The differential-pair claim (about 140 Ω) was wrong. With the model it is about 120–130 Ω, because halving s/h tightens the coupling.
  - Also fixed: the transmission coefficient into a 50 Ω load (0.816, not 1.184), and the tline sim caption, which now gives the real sim voltages.
- **q0530 and q0532 sims were double-terminated.** Both used `rl: 50` together with a parallel or Thevenin termination, so the 25 Ω load reflected, contradicting the "no reflection" caption. Changed to `rl: "open"`; the sims now show "Load matched".
- **q0525 TDR slope.** The page said the apparent impedance rises by R'ℓ/2. A ladder simulation shows it rises by about R'·x and reaches roughly Z0 + R'ℓ at the far end. Corrected.
