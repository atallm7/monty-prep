**Illustrative models:**

- **Breakeven on q0605/q0611/q0612:** already labelled illustrative in the plot title, the text and an UNCERTAIN callout. q0605 and q0611 now quote the example's own crossover (about 1,700 boards) in their short answers, which previously said "a few thousand boards".

**Most serious:**
- **q0605 follow-up logic:** the page said a fixture is "hard to amortise at 6,000 boards a year, as the crossover is around 1,700". Read literally, that contradicts the model. The page now explains the real reason: with quarterly ECOs, each fixture revision only sees about 1,500 boards, below the crossover.
- **q0597 second-side reflow:** the page said "heavy parts are placed on the second pass only if surface tension can hold them". That confuses the sides. Surface tension must hold the inverted *first-side* parts, and heavy parts go on the second side.
- **q0608 false-call arithmetic:**
  - A quiz explanation said 0.6 false calls per board means "more than half of good boards need review". The Poisson figure is 1 − e^−0.6 = 45%.
  - The deep section said 1 call per board means "every good board needs review". The actual figure is 63%.
- **q0599 spoken answer:** it advised reflowing "the more critical or finer-pitch side first". Replaced it with the standard practice: light, small parts on the first side.
- **q0594:**
  - The throughput example claimed "10 mm rail on the long sides" yet got 6 boards. With that geometry it fits 9. It now uses rails on all sides, matching the deep section and follow-up.
  - The V-score figure drew scores stopping at the board edges. They now run across the whole panel, which is the reason V-scored boards must sit edge to edge.
- **q0612:** the spoken answer said "a thousand probes at 100 g push around 150 kg". The correct figure is 100 kg.
