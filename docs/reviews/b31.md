I recomputed every quiz answer (all 120 items) and every worked number in the technical sections with `node`. That covered the IPC-2221 width/current fits, sheet and spreading resistance, partial and plane inductance, via thermal and electrical resistance, Onderdonk fusing, constant-power collapse, the ripple and RMS currents, hot-loop L·di/dt and ring frequencies, the C·dv/dt injections, the divider coupling and time constants, and the capacitor SRF/|Z| tables. I screenshotted every figure and sim in the running app and read each one, including all the figures the writer never checked: q0534–q0541, q0545, q0546, q0548, q0551 and q0552. The schematics are electrically correct: FET pinouts, diode direction, Kelvin/CSA polarity and the thermal network. I checked every plot's equation against the text numbers and every sim's params against catalog.json. The validator reports 0 errors.

**Citations:** TI SPRA953 exists, but its title is *Semiconductor and IC Package Thermal Metrics*; I corrected it in q0540 and q0541. TI SLVA477 (*Basic Calculation of a Buck Converter's Power Stage*) is real, but it covers power-stage equations, not layout. It was cited for layout and input-capacitor *placement* on seven pages, so I reworded each citation to say what it actually supports. The Onderdonk reference no longer claims the equation is part of IPC-2221. The IPC-2221 constants (k = 0.048/0.024, exponents 0.44/0.725) and the IPC-2152 description are correct. The CISPR 32 reference in q0547 now notes that automotive uses CISPR 25.

**Most serious:**
- **q0543:** the drop for 5 mm traces was stated as 77 mV. It is 38 mV (4 squares each way); 77 mV is the 2.5 mm figure.
- **q0544:** the UNCERTAIN note had Onderdonk backwards ("upper bound on time"). The adiabatic equation gives the *shortest* time to melt, which is the *lowest* fusing current for a given time.
- **q0547:** the caption said reducing loop inductance lowers the ring's "frequency content". It raises the ring frequency (f ∝ 1/√L); only the overshoot and energy fall.
- **q0539:** the follow-up claimed a return via at 10 mm adds 7–10 nH, with the loop area going as pitch × plane spacing. For two vias the loop inductance is (µ0h/π)·ln(s/r): about 0.8 nH at 1 mm pitch and 1.7 nH at 10 mm for h = 1 mm.
- **q0551:** the sim caption told the learner to "raise the applied current", but there is no such control. The default pulse already drives the inductor to about 20 A of saturation.
