::: testing
Whether you can draw the lumped-element picture of a transmission line **and** explain what it predicts. The interviewer wants:

1. The per-unit-length $R'$, $L'$, $G'$, $C'$ ladder drawn correctly: series $R$ and $L$, *shunt* $G$ and $C$.
2. The formulas $Z_0 = \sqrt{(R'+j\omega L')/(G'+j\omega C')}$ and $\gamma=\alpha+j\beta$ and what falls out of them when $R'=G'=0$.
3. A physical reason for each element (where does $R'$ come from, where does $G'$ come from) and how an ideal line differs from a real one in practice: loss, dispersion, and a frequency-dependent $Z_0$.
:::

::: why
Every signal-integrity topic (reflections, loss, eye closure, equalization) is a consequence of this one model. If you can draw the ladder and say "ideal means $R'=G'=0$, real means loss that grows with frequency", you can reason about why a 10 Gb/s trace behaves differently from a 10 MHz one without memorizing rules. Interviewers like it because it separates people who only know "route 50 Ω" from people who know *why* 50 Ω is a number at all.
:::

::: concepts
- **Distributed element**: when the wire is a meaningful fraction of a wavelength, you can't lump it into one $R$, $L$, $C$. You slice it into infinitesimal sections. See [[q0709]].
- **Per-unit-length parameters**: $R'$ (Ω/m), $L'$ (H/m), $G'$ (S/m), $C'$ (F/m). They come from geometry and materials.
- **Characteristic impedance**: the ratio of voltage to current of a wave travelling one way on the line. See [[q0710]].
- **Propagation constant** $\gamma = \alpha + j\beta$: $\alpha$ is attenuation (Np/m), $\beta$ is phase (rad/m).
- **Lossless line**: $R'=G'=0$ gives $Z_0=\sqrt{L'/C'}$ and velocity $1/\sqrt{L'C'}$.
- **Dispersion**: different frequencies travelling at different speeds or with different loss, so edges smear.
:::

::: answer
"A transmission line is modelled as a ladder of tiny identical sections. In each slice of length $\Delta z$ I put a series resistance $R'\Delta z$ and series inductance $L'\Delta z$ in the signal path, and then a shunt capacitance $C'\Delta z$ and shunt conductance $G'\Delta z$ between the signal and the return conductor. Stack infinitely many of those and you get the real line.

Where they come from: $L'$ is the magnetic energy stored by the current loop between signal and return, $C'$ is the electric field energy between the conductors, $R'$ is the finite conductivity of the copper, and $G'$ is the dielectric: its polarization loss and a bit of leakage.

An ideal line has $R'$ and $G'$ equal to zero. Then $Z_0 = \sqrt{L'/C'}$, it's purely real and constant, the velocity is $1/\sqrt{L'C'}$ at every frequency, and nothing is lost. A real line has both loss terms. $R'$ rises with $\sqrt f$ from the skin effect, $G'$ rises with $f$ from dielectric loss, so the line attenuates high frequencies more, which rounds edges, and the impedance picks up a small frequency dependence. At low frequency, where $R'$ beats $\omega L'$, it even stops looking like a clean 50 Ω line and behaves like a lossy RC."
:::

::: technical
**Model.** One section of length $\Delta z$: series impedance $Z'\Delta z = (R' + j\omega L')\Delta z$, shunt admittance $Y'\Delta z = (G' + j\omega C')\Delta z$.

**Telegrapher's equations.** Taking $\Delta z\to 0$:
$$\frac{\partial v}{\partial z} = -R' i - L'\frac{\partial i}{\partial t},\qquad \frac{\partial i}{\partial z} = -G' v - C'\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}$$

**Phasor solution.** Waves $e^{\mp\gamma z}$ with
$$\gamma = \sqrt{(R'+j\omega L')(G'+j\omega C')} = \alpha + j\beta,\qquad Z_0=\sqrt{\frac{R'+j\omega L'}{G'+j\omega C'}}$$

**Ideal ($R'=G'=0$):** $\gamma = j\omega\sqrt{L'C'}$, so $\alpha=0$, $\beta=\omega\sqrt{L'C'}$, $v_p = 1/\sqrt{L'C'}$, $Z_0=\sqrt{L'/C'}$ real and frequency independent. Delay per length $=\sqrt{L'C'}$.

**Low-loss ($R'\ll\omega L'$, $G'\ll\omega C'$):**
$$\alpha \approx \frac{R'}{2Z_0} + \frac{G' Z_0}{2},\qquad Z_0\approx\sqrt{L'/C'}$$
The first term is conductor loss, the second is dielectric loss.

**Real behaviour.** $R'(f)\approx R'_{dc}$ at low $f$ and $\propto\sqrt f$ above the skin-effect corner (see [[q0725]]). $G'=\omega C'\tan\delta$, so dielectric loss grows roughly $\propto f$ ([[q0723]]). $\varepsilon_r$ itself drifts down slowly with frequency in FR-4, which makes the line mildly dispersive. Below $f\approx R'/(2\pi L')$ the line is RC-dominated and $|Z_0|\approx\sqrt{R'/\omega C'}$ with a phase of about $-45^\circ$.

**Numbers for a typical 50 Ω FR-4 microstrip** ($w\approx 0.39$ mm over 0.2 mm dielectric, $\varepsilon_{eff}\approx3.26$): $L'\approx 301$ nH/m (7.6 nH/in), $C'\approx 120$ pF/m (3.1 pF/in), delay $\approx 153$ ps/in, $R'_{dc}\approx 1.2$ Ω/m for 35 µm copper.

> [!UNCERTAIN]
> Closed-form $Z_0$ and $\varepsilon_{eff}$ for microstrip (Hammerstad-type formulas) are accurate to a few percent. Use a field solver or your fab's stackup tool for real boards.
:::

::: short
"A real transmission line is a ladder of small sections, each with a series $R'$ and $L'$ and a shunt $G'$ and $C'$ per unit length. $L'$ and $C'$ are the stored magnetic and electric energy and set $Z_0=\sqrt{L'/C'}$ and the speed. $R'$ is copper loss, $G'$ is dielectric loss. The ideal line sets $R'$ and $G'$ to zero, so no loss, no dispersion, and a constant real $Z_0$. A real one attenuates high frequencies more, from skin effect and dielectric loss, so edges slow down and round off."
:::

::: long
"I'd draw one section of the line: a series resistor and a series inductor in the top path, then a capacitor and a conductance from the top path down to the return. Each value is per unit length times $\Delta z$. Chain a lot of these and that's the line.

Each element has a physical source. $L'$ is the loop inductance of signal plus return path, set by geometry, so a wider trace over thinner dielectric has less. $C'$ is the capacitance between trace and plane, which goes up with a wider trace or higher $\varepsilon_r$. $R'$ is the copper resistance, which gets worse with frequency because current crowds into a skin. $G'$ is the dielectric: the polarization of the material lags the field, which dissipates power, and it's proportional to $\omega C'\tan\delta$.

The line's behaviour falls out of two formulas. The characteristic impedance is $\sqrt{(R'+j\omega L')/(G'+j\omega C')}$ and the propagation constant is $\sqrt{(R'+j\omega L')(G'+j\omega C')}$.

For the ideal line, $R'$ and $G'$ are zero. Then $Z_0=\sqrt{L'/C'}$ is real and the same at every frequency, the signal moves at $1/\sqrt{L'C'}$ at every frequency, and a pulse arrives with exactly its original shape, just delayed.

Real lines differ in three ways. They attenuate, with a loss that rises with frequency. They disperse, since loss and effective permittivity vary with frequency so the edge rounds and spreads. And $Z_0$ becomes slightly complex and frequency dependent, mostly noticeable at low frequency where $R'$ is comparable to $\omega L'$. On a PCB for most digital edges I treat $Z_0$ as constant and add loss on top."
:::

::: terms
- **RLGC**: the four per-unit-length parameters of a transmission line.
- **Telegrapher's equations**: the coupled PDEs for $v(z,t)$ and $i(z,t)$ on the line.
- **Characteristic impedance** $Z_0$: $\sqrt{Z'/Y'}$, the impedance a long line presents to a wave.
- **Propagation constant** $\gamma=\alpha+j\beta$: attenuation plus phase per unit length.
- **Loss tangent** $\tan\delta$: ratio of dielectric loss to stored energy in the dielectric.
- **Skin effect**: current crowding to the conductor surface at high frequency, which raises $R'$.
- **Dispersion**: frequency-dependent velocity or loss that distorts the pulse shape.
- **Lossless line**: the $R'=G'=0$ idealization.
:::

::: exceptional
**An average answer** draws four components and says "ideal has no loss".

**An exceptional answer**:
- Puts $R'$ and $L'$ in **series** and $G'$ and $C'$ in **shunt**, and says why: current flows *along* the conductors, field energy is stored *between* them.
- Writes $Z_0$ and $\gamma$ and shows $\sqrt{L'/C'}$ and $1/\sqrt{L'C'}$ drop out when $R'=G'=0$.
- Gives physical origins for each element, including that $R'\propto\sqrt f$ and $G'\propto f$.
- Notes the low-frequency RC regime where $Z_0$ is not 50 Ω at all.
- Gives real numbers: about 7.6 nH/in and 3.1 pF/in for a 50 Ω FR-4 trace, about 150 ps/in.
- Connects to consequences: attenuation, edge rounding, intersymbol interference.
:::

::: mistakes
- **Putting $C$ and $G$ in series, or $R$ and $L$ in shunt.** Series elements carry the signal current along the line, shunt elements leak between the conductors.
- **Saying the ideal line has no impedance.** It has $Z_0=\sqrt{L'/C'}$, real, with no loss. "Lossless" does not mean "zero impedance".
- **Quoting $Z_0=\sqrt{L/C}$ with total $L$ and $C$ of the whole trace.** It works because the length cancels, but it's per-unit-length parameters that define the line.
- **Thinking $R'$ is the DC resistance at all frequencies.** Skin effect makes it grow as $\sqrt f$.
- **Treating $G'$ as the reciprocal of $R'$.** $G'$ is shunt conductance between conductors, a separate loss mechanism with different frequency dependence.
- **Calling a 50 Ω line a 50 Ω resistor.** It looks like 50 Ω only to a travelling wave, and only while the line is long enough that reflections haven't returned.
:::

::: deep
## Why a ladder of tiny sections

Take a wire pair 10 cm long carrying a 1 ns edge. In FR-4 the edge physically occupies about 17 cm (1 ns × 1.66 × 10⁸ m/s), so the line is a large fraction of the edge length and the voltage at one end is not the voltage at the other end at the same instant. A single lumped $L$ and $C$ cannot capture that, but a chain of $N$ small sections can. Each slice is short enough to be lumped, and the delay builds up from the chain. See [[q0709]] for when this matters.

Each element has a clean origin:

| Element | Physical origin | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| $L'$ | Magnetic energy of the signal/return loop | Geometry: trace height above the plane, width |
| $C'$ | Electric field energy between trace and plane | Geometry, $\varepsilon_r$ |
| $R'$ | Finite conductivity of copper, skin effect, roughness | $\sqrt f$ above the skin corner |
| $G'$ | Dielectric polarization loss plus leakage | $\omega C'\tan\delta$, so $\propto f$ |

## Deriving $Z_0$ and $\gamma$

Apply KVL and KCL to one section, divide by $\Delta z$ and let it shrink. In phasor form:
$$\frac{dV}{dz} = -(R'+j\omega L')I,\qquad \frac{dI}{dz}=-(G'+j\omega C')V$$
Differentiating one and substituting the other gives $\frac{d^2V}{dz^2}=\gamma^2V$ with $\gamma^2=(R'+j\omega L')(G'+j\omega C')$. The solution is a forward wave $V^+e^{-\gamma z}$ plus a backward wave $V^-e^{+\gamma z}$, and the ratio of $V^+$ to its current is $Z_0=\sqrt{(R'+j\omega L')/(G'+j\omega C')}$.

> [!TIP]
> If you forget the formula in an interview, derive it in one line: the line is a series impedance divided by a shunt admittance, and the ratio of the two square-roots is $Z_0$; their product is $\gamma^2$.

## Ideal vs real, side by side

| Property | Ideal ($R'=G'=0$) | Real |
|---|---|---|
| $Z_0$ | $\sqrt{L'/C'}$, real, constant | $\approx\sqrt{L'/C'}$ above ~10 MHz, complex and large below |
| Attenuation $\alpha$ | 0 | $\approx R'/2Z_0 + G'Z_0/2$, grows with $f$ |
| Velocity | $1/\sqrt{L'C'}$, constant | Slightly frequency dependent (dispersion) |
| Pulse shape | Arrives identical, delayed | Edge slows and rounds, ISI appears |
| Reflection coefficient | Exactly $(Z_L-Z_0)/(Z_L+Z_0)$ | Same, with an extra factor $e^{-2\alpha\ell}$ on round trips |

## Worked example: where is the line "not 50 Ω"?

For the FR-4 microstrip above: $R'_{dc}=\rho/(wt)=1.68\times10^{-8}/(0.39\text{ mm}\times35\text{ µm})\approx1.23$ Ω/m and $L'\approx301$ nH/m. The corner where $R'=\omega L'$ is
$$f=\frac{R'}{2\pi L'}=\frac{1.23}{2\pi\cdot301\times10^{-9}}\approx 650\text{ kHz}$$
Below that, the magnitude of $Z_0$ is set by the *RC* behaviour, which is why a long thin trace carrying audio looks like a lossy RC wire and not a 50 Ω line. Plotting $|Z_0|$ from the full formula (with $R'$ rising as $\sqrt{1+f/f_s}$ and $G'=\omega C'\tan\delta$, $\tan\delta=0.02$):

## What real loss does to a signal

Total attenuation $A(f)=e^{-\alpha(f)\ell}$. Because $\alpha$ grows with $f$, the line is a low-pass filter whose roll-off steepens with length. A 1 ns edge launched into a lossy line arrives with its high-frequency content eaten, so it looks slower. Longer lines, faster data and cheaper dielectric all push toward more loss. The next questions go through it: loss sources in [[q0722]], dielectric detail in [[q0723]], skin effect in [[q0725]], and how to claw back with equalization in [[q0726]].

::: applications
- **PCB microstrip/stripline on FR-4 vs low-loss laminate**: the choice between them is largely a $\tan\delta$ ($G'$) decision at multi-gigabit rates.
- **Coax cables (RG-58, RG-6)**: datasheets quote attenuation in dB per 100 ft vs frequency, which is the $R'$ and $G'$ model compressed into one curve.
- **On-chip interconnect**: thin, narrow wires have huge $R'$, so long global wires are RC lines and need repeaters.
- **Twisted pair (Ethernet, CAN)**: same model, with $L'$ and $C'$ set by wire spacing.
- **SPICE and S-parameter models**: lossy T-line elements in simulators are RLGC with frequency-dependent $R(f)$ and $G(f)$, or measured S-parameters.
:::

::: assumptions
- **Uniform line**: $R'$, $L'$, $G'$, $C'$ constant along the length. Real boards have vias, connectors and stubs that break this.
- **TEM (or quasi-TEM) propagation**: microstrip is quasi-TEM, so $\varepsilon_{eff}$ changes slightly with frequency.
- **Linear and time-invariant**: no nonlinear dielectric or ferrite effects.
- **Skin-effect model for $R'(f)$** is approximate. Surface roughness adds further loss, and vendors' copper-roughness treatments change it.
- **Constant $\tan\delta$** is a convenient simplification. Real laminates show $\tan\delta$ varying with frequency, and the datasheet value is usually at one frequency.

If the interviewer says "lossless", they want $Z_0=\sqrt{L'/C'}$ and no $\alpha$. If they say "low-loss", they want $\alpha\approx R'/2Z_0+G'Z_0/2$.
:::

::: followups
### Why is the characteristic impedance $\sqrt{L'/C'}$ for the ideal line?
A forward wave has $V/I=Z_0$. Per unit length, the line stores magnetic energy $\tfrac12L'I^2$ and electric energy $\tfrac12C'V^2$. For a wave to propagate with no reflection those two must match, $L'I^2=C'V^2$, which gives $V/I=\sqrt{L'/C'}$. For the FR-4 example, $\sqrt{301\text{ nH}/120.5\text{ pF}}=50$ Ω.

### What is the delay of the 50 Ω FR-4 microstrip per inch, and per 10 cm?
$t_d=\sqrt{L'C'}=\sqrt{301\text{ nH/m}\times120.5\text{ pF/m}}=6.02$ ns/m, so about 153 ps/in and about 0.60 ns per 10 cm. That's a velocity of $c/\sqrt{\varepsilon_{eff}}\approx0.55c$ with $\varepsilon_{eff}\approx3.26$.

### Which of $R'$ and $G'$ dominates loss on a PCB trace?
It depends on frequency and geometry. Conductor loss ($\propto\sqrt f$) usually dominates below a few hundred MHz to about 1 GHz, and dielectric loss ($\propto f$) takes over above that in FR-4. With low-loss laminate ($\tan\delta\approx0.004$), conductor loss stays dominant to much higher frequency. See [[q0722]].

### Does a lossy line still have a well-defined $Z_0$?
Yes, but complex: $Z_0=\sqrt{(R'+j\omega L')/(G'+j\omega C')}$. In the low-loss regime the imaginary part is tiny and $Z_0\approx\sqrt{L'/C'}$. A first-order correction is $Z_0\approx\sqrt{L'/C'}\,[1 - j(R'/\omega L' - G'/\omega C')/2]$.

### Scenario: your scope shows a 1 ns edge launched into a 1 m cable arriving with a 3 ns rise time. What do you suspect?
Loss and dispersion in the cable. Estimate the bandwidth of the 1 ns edge at $0.35/t_r\approx350$ MHz. If the cable loses a few dB at 350 MHz, with more loss at higher harmonics, the received edge slows. First check the cable spec attenuation at 350 MHz, then try a shorter cable and a lower-loss type. Also check that source and load terminations aren't adding rounding from capacitive loading.

### Harder: can $R'$ and $G'$ ever make a line distortionless?
Yes, the Heaviside condition $R'/L'=G'/C'$ makes $\alpha$ frequency independent and $v_p$ constant, so the line is lossy but doesn't distort. Real lines have $R'/L'\gg G'/C'$, which is why historic telephone lines added loading coils (extra $L$) to approach it. On PCBs it's not practical, so we equalize instead.
:::

::: interpretations
"Draw real TL distributed model" means the lumped RLGC ladder. Some candidates also draw the **lossless LC ladder** first and then add $R$ and $G$ to show the difference, which is a good way to structure the answer. "How do they differ between ideal vs real" asks about the components: ideal has only $L'$ and $C'$, real adds $R'$ and $G'$ (and makes them frequency dependent).
