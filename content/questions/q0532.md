::: testing
Whether you can **list and draw the standard termination networks** (series, parallel, Thevenin/split, AC/RC, diode clamp, differential, $V_{TT}$/ODT), give each one's design equation, and compare them on the axes interviewers care about: DC power, signal swing and noise margin, delay/edge impact, component count and where it must sit. The sub-skill is picking the right one for a topology instead of reciting a list.
:::

::: why
Every interface datasheet picks a termination scheme, and layout reviewers expect you to know why. Getting it wrong costs power (parallel to ground on a 3.3 V bus), signal integrity (series on multi-drop) or compliance (differential links). It also connects the theory of matching ([[q0714]]) to concrete part values.
:::

::: concepts
- **Matching condition**: terminate in $Z_0$ so $\Gamma=0$. See [[q0714]].
- **Series (source) termination**: $R_s=Z_0-R_{out}$. See [[q0716]].
- **Parallel (end) termination**: $R_T=Z_0$ to a reference. See [[q0717]].
- **Thevenin (split) termination**: two resistors, parallel value $Z_0$, bias at $V_{TH}$.
- **AC termination**: $R=Z_0$ in series with a capacitor; no DC current.
- **Differential termination**: resistor across the pair.
:::

::: answer
"The main ones are these. Series termination: a resistor at the driver so that driver impedance plus resistor equals Z0. It uses no DC power and only works with the receiver at the end. Parallel termination: a resistor equal to Z0 from the end of the line to a reference, ground or a mid-rail VTT; it gives a clean wave along the whole line, but a CMOS driver has to drive that DC load, so power is high. Thevenin or split termination: two resistors, one to the supply and one to ground, with a parallel value of Z0, so it sets a DC bias at the receiver too, say 100 and 100 ohms for 50 ohms at half the supply. It still burns DC power. AC termination: Z0 in series with a capacitor, so there's no DC current but the capacitor has to be large enough, around 100 picofarads to a nanofarad. Differential termination: a resistor across the pair, typically 100 ohms, often on-die. Then there are diode clamps, Schottky diodes to the rails that don't match but limit overshoot, and DDR's VTT termination and on-die termination.

The choice is topology and power: series for point-to-point where I want low power, parallel or Thevenin for multi-drop or when I need a clean wave all along the line, AC when DC power matters, differential for pairs."
:::

::: technical
**Design equations** ($Z_0$ line impedance, $R_{out}$ driver impedance, $V_{CC}$ supply):
| Network | Values | DC power (high state, 3.3 V, $Z_0=50$ Ω) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| None | – | 0 | ok only if electrically short |
| Series | $R_s=Z_0-R_{out}$ (e.g. 30 Ω for 20 Ω driver) | ≈0 | half-step launched; receiver at end; edge slightly slower |
| Parallel to GND | $R_T=Z_0$ | $V^2/R=218$ mW (≈109 mW avg) | full swing reduced to $V\,R_T/(R_T+R_{out})$ |
| Parallel to $V_{TT}$ ($V_{CC}/2$) | $R_T=Z_0$ | ≈ $(V_{CC}/2)^2/R_T$ = 54 mW | DDR style; needs a $V_{TT}$ regulator |
| Thevenin/split | $R_1=Z_0V_{CC}/V_{TH}$, $R_2=Z_0V_{CC}/(V_{CC}-V_{TH})$ | $V_{CC}^2/(R_1+R_2)$ = 54 mW at 100/100 | sets bias $V_{TH}$ |
| AC (RC) | $R=Z_0$, $C\gtrsim$ a few × round-trip/R | ≈0 DC; ≈$CV^2f$ dynamic | $RC\gtrsim3\times2t_d$ heuristic |
| Diode clamp | Schottky to rails | ≈0 | clamps overshoot only; not a match |
| Differential | $R=Z_{diff}$ (100 Ω) across the pair; or 2×50 Ω + bypass cap to ground | small | often on-die |
| ODT/ZQ | on-die programmable (e.g. 40–120 Ω) | per spec | no external parts |

**Thevenin example.** $V_{CC}=3.3$ V, want $V_{TH}=1.65$ V, $Z_0=50$ Ω: $R_1=R_2=100$ Ω; static current $3.3/200=16.5$ mA, 54 mW. For $V_{TH}=1.5$ V: $R_1=110$ Ω, $R_2=91.7$ Ω.

**AC example.** $R=50$ Ω, $C=100$ pF: $\tau=5$ ns, ≥ 3× a 1 ns round trip. Dynamic power $\approx CV^2f_{toggle}$: at 50 MHz, 3.3 V, 100 pF ≈ 54 mW, so not free.

**Selection.** Point-to-point, low power: series. Multi-drop or clean mid-line wave: parallel/Thevenin at the end. Power-sensitive with long lines: AC. Differential: resistor across the pair. Memory: $V_{TT}$/ODT per the standard.
:::

::: short
"The common termination networks are: series, a resistor at the source so $R_{out}+R_s=Z_0$, no DC power; parallel, $Z_0$ from the end of the line to ground or $V_{TT}$, which costs DC power; Thevenin, two resistors to the rails with parallel value $Z_0$, which also sets a bias; AC, $Z_0$ in series with a capacitor, no DC current; diode clamps for overshoot; and a differential resistor across a pair, often on-die. I pick by topology and power: series for point-to-point, parallel or Thevenin for multi-drop, differential for pairs."
:::

::: long
"There are about six I'd describe.

Series termination is a resistor at the driver so that the driver's output impedance plus the resistor equals the line impedance. A 20 ohm driver on a 50 ohm line needs about 30 ohms. It draws no DC current, launches half the step, and the full voltage appears at the far end after the reflection; it only works when the receiver is at the end of the line.

Parallel termination is a resistor equal to Z0 from the end of the line to ground, or to a mid-rail voltage called VTT as in DDR. It absorbs the wave on arrival so the whole line sees the clean step, but the driver has to supply a DC current: 3.3 volts into 50 ohms is 66 milliamps, which is around 100 milliwatts on average.

Thevenin or split termination uses two resistors, one to the supply and one to ground, whose parallel value is Z0. For half the supply that's 100 ohms each, which gives 50 ohms and a bias at 1.65 volts. It halves the DC load compared with a single 50 ohm to ground, but still burns 54 milliwatts.

AC termination is Z0 in series with a capacitor, say 100 picofarads, so there's no DC current. The capacitor needs a time constant a few times the round-trip delay. It's not free, though: its dynamic power is C V squared f.

Diode terminations, Schottky diodes to the rails, don't match the line, but clamp overshoot and undershoot.

For differential pairs it's a resistor across the pair, typically 100 ohms, often on-die, or two 50 ohm resistors with a capacitor to ground for the common mode.

How I choose: point-to-point and low power, series. Multi-drop, parallel or Thevenin at the end. Power-critical and long, AC. Pairs, differential. And memory interfaces follow the standard with VTT or on-die termination."
:::

::: terms
- **Series termination**: source resistor that completes the match.
- **Parallel termination**: end resistor to a reference.
- **Thevenin termination**: resistor divider to rails whose parallel value is $Z_0$.
- **AC termination**: $R$ in series with $C$ so there is no DC path.
- **$V_{TT}$**: termination voltage, usually $V_{DD}/2$.
- **Clamp (diode) termination**: diodes to the rails limiting overshoot.
- **Differential termination**: resistor across the pair, $R=Z_{diff}$.
- **ODT**: on-die termination, programmable resistors in the chip.
:::

::: exceptional
An average answer says "series and parallel". An exceptional answer:
- Lists **Thevenin, AC, diode, differential, $V_{TT}$/ODT** and gives **design equations** with a numeric example.
- Compares **DC power, swing, delay, parts count, placement** in a table.
- Notes **hidden costs**: AC termination's $CV^2f$, parallel's reduced swing with a resistive driver, Thevenin bias set-point.
- Matches **network to topology** (point-to-point, multi-drop, fly-by, pairs).
- Mentions **ODT/ZQ calibration** and that real driver impedance varies.
:::

::: mistakes
- **Parallel termination to ground on a 3.3 V CMOS bus** without checking driver current and the reduced high level ($V_{OH}$ drops).
- **Thevenin with mismatched $V_{TH}$.** The bias must match the receiver's input range; 100/100 gives mid-rail.
- **AC termination with too small a capacitor.** Behaves like an open at the signal's low frequencies and gives a droop/baseline wander.
- **Calling AC termination free.** The capacitor charges every edge: $CV^2f$.
- **Using diode clamps as a termination.** They limit overshoot but don't remove ringing at small amplitudes.
- **Using series termination on a bus with several receivers.**
- **Ignoring driver output impedance variation** (±20–30%), which shifts $R_s$.
:::

## Design equations and examples

**Series.** $R_s=Z_0-R_{out}$. A 20 Ω driver needs 30 Ω (33 Ω standard). Launched step: $V\cdot Z_0/(R_{out}+R_s+Z_0)=V/2$. Total delay to a clean full-level at the load: $t_d$; the source sees the half-level step for $2t_d$.

**Parallel.** $R_T=Z_0$ to ground: a resistive divider with the driver: high level $V_{OH}=V\cdot\dfrac{R_T}{R_T+R_{out}}=3.3\times\dfrac{50}{70}=2.36$ V for a 20 Ω driver. That reduces noise margin and costs 66 mA at 3.3 V.

**Thevenin.** $R_1\parallel R_2=Z_0$ and $V_{TH}=V_{CC}R_2/(R_1+R_2)$ give $R_1=Z_0V_{CC}/V_{TH}$, $R_2=Z_0V_{CC}/(V_{CC}-V_{TH})$. For $V_{TH}=V_{CC}/2$: $R_1=R_2=2Z_0=100$ Ω. Static current 16.5 mA at 3.3 V (54 mW); driver current is shared: half the swing for each state.

**AC.** Choose $R=Z_0$ and $C$ such that $RC$ is several times $2t_d$ so the capacitor looks like a short during the edge. Example: $2t_d=1$ ns, $R=50$ Ω, $C=100$ pF: $\tau=5$ ns. The cost: current only at transitions, but $P\approx CV^2f_{tog}$; at 50 MHz toggling, 3.3 V, 100 pF this is 54 mW (comparable to Thevenin) so AC termination saves power only with low toggle rates or small $C$.

> [!UNCERTAIN]
> The "$RC\ge3\times$ round-trip" guideline for AC termination is a heuristic; textbooks give factors from 2 to 10 depending on tolerance for droop. Check by simulation with the real data pattern.

**Differential.** For an interface with $Z_{diff}=100$ Ω, a 100 Ω resistor across the pair; alternatively two 50 Ω resistors with a capacitor from their midpoint to ground to terminate the common mode (as in some LVDS/HDMI receivers).

**$V_{TT}$ and ODT.** DDR address/command nets terminate with $\approx$ 40–50 Ω to $V_{TT}=V_{DDQ}/2$ (source/sink current rated regulator), data lines use on-die termination (ODT) selected by mode registers with calibration resistors (ZQ) to hold the value over PVT.

## Comparison

Logic levels at the receiver for a 20 Ω CMOS driver on 3.3 V (DC, resistive divider with the termination):

| Termination | $V_{OH}$ | $V_{OL}$ | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| None, series, AC | 3.30 V | 0 V | no DC load |
| 50 Ω to ground | 2.36 V | 0 V | high level eroded, low level intact |
| 100/100 Thevenin ($V_{TH}=1.65$ V) | 2.83 V | 0.47 V | levels symmetric around $V_{TH}$ |
| 50 Ω to $V_{TT}=1.65$ V | 2.83 V | 0.47 V | same levels as Thevenin, but needs a $V_{TT}$ rail |

Termination to ground costs high-level margin; terminations to mid-rail share the loss between the two levels, at the price of reduced swing (about 2.4 V instead of 3.3 V).

| Network | DC power | Swing/margin | Delay impact | Parts | Placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Series | ≈0 | full swing at end | half-step first, adds $RC$ with load C | 1 R | at source |
| Parallel (GND) | high | reduced $V_{OH}$ | none | 1 R | at end |
| Parallel ($V_{TT}$) | moderate | symmetrical ±$V/2$ | none | 1 R + rail | at end |
| Thevenin | moderate | bias set by $V_{TH}$ | none | 2 R | at end |
| AC | ≈0 DC, $CV^2f$ | full swing; droop with low-frequency data | none | R + C | at end |
| Diode | ≈0 | clamps to rails ± 0.3 V | none | 2 diodes | at end |
| Differential | small | per standard | none | 1 R | at receiver |

## Real hardware behaviour

- **Driver impedance varies** with voltage swing, temperature and process, so use the IBIS $I$–$V$ curve at mid-swing to set $R_s$, and verify on the bench with a scope at the receiver.
- **DDR $V_{TT}$** regulators must sink and source current (tracking $V_{DDQ}/2$); their placement and decoupling matter.
- **ODT** is calibrated by ZQ pin resistors (e.g. 240 Ω ±1% in many DDR parts) to hold the termination over PVT.
:::

::: applications
- **Clock buffers and MCU outputs**: 22–33 Ω series resistors.
- **DDR3/DDR4 address/command**: $V_{TT}$ parallel termination at the fly-by end.
- **Legacy ECL/PECL**: Thevenin terminations to $V_{CC}-2$ V.
- **LVDS/HDMI/PCIe receivers**: differential termination (100 Ω) on-die.
- **Long cables and backplanes**: AC termination to avoid DC load, or diode clamps for protection.
:::

::: assumptions
- Linear driver of known $R_{out}$ and ideal resistors; real parts add ESL and tolerance.
- Values use $Z_0=50$ Ω and 3.3 V; other values scale the same way.
- "Termination networks" covers board-level discrete networks; ODT and driver calibration are treated as an alternative.
- Power numbers use 50% duty cycle where stated and neglect the driver's own dynamic power.
:::

::: followups
### Why does a parallel termination to ground lower the high level?
The driver's output impedance and the termination form a divider: $V_{OH}=V\cdot R_T/(R_T+R_{out})$. With 20 Ω into 50 Ω that is 2.36 V from a 3.3 V rail, which erodes noise margin and can fall below $V_{IH}$ for some receivers. That's why mid-rail ($V_{TT}$) and Thevenin variants were invented.

### How do you choose the capacitor for AC termination?
Large enough that $RC\gg2t_d$ so that it acts as a short for the edge and the line sees $R=Z_0$, but small enough to limit dynamic power ($CV^2f$) and avoid slow recovery. 100 pF–1 nF is typical. I'd verify with the real data pattern, because long runs of 1s let the capacitor charge and the level droop.

### Design a Thevenin termination for a 5 V TTL-level bus with $Z_0=60$ Ω and $V_{TH}=2.5$ V.
$R_1=Z_0V_{CC}/V_{TH}=60\times5/2.5=120$ Ω and $R_2=Z_0V_{CC}/(V_{CC}-V_{TH})=120$ Ω. Static current $5/240=20.8$ mA, 104 mW.

### What if the driver impedance is unknown?
Measure it: drive a known load and compare the voltages, or use the IBIS model. Then pick $R_s$ as $Z_0-R_{out}$; start with 33 Ω for 50 Ω lines and tune with the scope at the receiver, or use drivers with calibrated output impedance.

### When is a diode clamp better than a resistor?
When the problem is overshoot on a high-impedance input from occasional reflections and you can't afford DC power, or as protection. It doesn't absorb the wave, so ringing below the clamp threshold remains, and the diodes' capacitance loads the line.
:::
