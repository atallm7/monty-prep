::: testing
Whether you can trace real current paths through the circuit in each state and apply KCL at the output node, instead of memorizing "inductor charges, capacitor discharges". The trap is the capacitor: its current direction is *not* tied to the on/off state. A strong answer says the inductor current flows the same way (switch node toward output) in both states, and the capacitor current depends on whether the inductor current is above or below the load current.
:::

::: why
Current direction is the basis of every loss, ripple and reverse-current question that follows: which device carries current in which state, why the diode must point the way it does, how a synchronous buck can run backwards in forced-CCM, and what the input capacitor does. Getting directions wrong also leads to wrong signs in the capacitor charge-balance argument, and the common wrong claim that "the capacitor discharges during the on state."
:::

::: concepts
- **Conventional current**: positive charge flow from positive to negative; arrows follow it. See [[q0001]].
- **KCL at the output node**: $i_L = i_C + i_{load}$. See [[q0004]].
- **Inductor continuity**: current cannot change instantly, so it continues in the same direction when the FET opens. See [[q0043]].
- **Capacitor law** $i_C = C\,dv/dt$: positive $i_C$ (into the cap) means voltage rising. See [[q0025]].
- **Two switch states** on (high-side FET) and off (diode or low-side FET). See [[q0174]].
:::

::: answer
"The inductor current always flows left to right, from the switch node toward the output, in both states. That's what 'continuous conduction' means. In the on state it comes from the input through the high-side FET, and the inductor current is rising. In the off state the high-side FET is open, but the inductor won't let the current change instantly, so the current keeps flowing the same direction, now pulled up from ground through the diode or the low-side FET, and it falls.

The capacitor is different, because its current depends on KCL at the output node: inductor current equals capacitor current plus load current. Whenever the inductor current is above the load current, the extra goes into the capacitor, so it charges, and when it's below, the load pulls current out of the capacitor and it discharges. In steady state that means the capacitor charges for roughly the last half of the on state and the first part of the off state, and discharges for the rest. So the capacitor current is positive about half the time regardless of the switch states; it flips at the middle of each inductor ramp, not at the switching edge."
:::

::: technical
Define positive $i_L$ from the switch node through $L$ to the output node, and positive $i_C$ flowing down into the capacitor's positive plate. KCL at the output node: $i_L = i_C + i_{load}$, so $i_C = i_L - i_{load}$.

**On state** ($0<t<DT$): $V_{SW}=V_{in}$. Path: $V_{in}\to$ high-side FET $\to$ SW $\to L\to V_{out}\to$ load/capacitor $\to$ ground $\to V_{in}$. $v_L = V_{in}-V_{out}>0$ so $di_L/dt>0$. Rising current from 1.49 A to 2.51 A in the example.

**Off state** ($DT<t<T$): $V_{SW}\approx0$ (minus $V_f$). Path: ground $\to$ diode (or low-side FET) $\to$ SW $\to L\to V_{out}\to$ load/capacitor $\to$ ground. $v_L=-V_{out}<0$, $di_L/dt<0$, so the current falls from 2.51 A to 1.49 A but flows in the *same* direction.

**Capacitor.** $i_C = i_L - I_{out}$ (with $I_{out}=2$ A):
- $i_L<2$ A (valley to the middle of the on ramp, and the last part of the off ramp): $i_C<0$, discharging, $v_C$ falling.
- $i_L>2$ A (second half of the on ramp, first half of the off ramp): $i_C>0$, charging, $v_C$ rising.
The zero crossings are at $t = DT/2 = 275$ ns and $t=DT + (1-D)T/2 = 1.275\ \mu s$. Charging lasts exactly $T/2=1$ µs and discharging lasts $T/2$ too.

**Exceptions.** In DCM, $i_L = 0$ for part of the cycle and the capacitor alone supplies the load. In a synchronous buck at light load with forced CCM the average inductor current can be smaller than the ripple/2, so $i_L$ goes negative (output toward the switch node) for part of the cycle.
:::

::: short
"The inductor current flows from the switch node toward the output in both states; it ramps up when the high-side FET is on and the current comes from the input, and it ramps down during the off state, with the current coming up through the diode or low-side FET from ground. The capacitor current is $i_L$ minus the load current, so it's positive, charging, when the inductor current is above the average and negative, discharging, when it's below, and the sign change happens mid-ramp, not at the switching edge."
:::

::: long
"Let me define the directions first. The inductor current positive direction is from the switch node through the inductor to the output. The capacitor current is positive going down into the capacitor, charging it.

On state: the high-side FET connects the switch node to the input. The inductor sees the input minus the output, a positive 8.7 V in my example, so the current ramps up. The current path is input, high-side FET, inductor, then it splits into the load and the capacitor, and returns through ground to the input.

Off state: the high-side FET opens and the inductor current can't stop instantly, so the voltage across the inductor reverses, the switch node swings down until the diode or the low-side FET picks up the current. The inductor current continues in exactly the same direction, from the switch node to the output, but now it's ramping down. The current path is ground, through the diode, the inductor, and the load, and back to ground; the input source isn't in the loop at all.

The capacitor depends on KCL at the output node. The current coming through the inductor splits into the load, which takes the DC average, and the capacitor, which takes the difference. If the inductor current is above 2 A, the extra goes into the capacitor, which charges. If it's below, the capacitor discharges to make up the shortfall. So the capacitor is charging in the second half of the on time and the first part of the off time, and discharging the rest. Half the period each, and the changeover is where the inductor current crosses the average."
:::

::: terms
- **Conventional current**: direction of positive charge flow; used for all arrows.
- **CCM**: continuous conduction; the inductor current stays positive through the cycle.
- **KCL at the output node**: the inductor current equals the capacitor current plus load current.
- **Freewheeling**: the inductor current continuing through the diode/low-side FET during the off state.
- **Charge balance**: the average capacitor current is zero in steady state.
- **Ripple current**: the AC component of the inductor current, which flows through the capacitor.
- **Forced CCM**: synchronous mode where the inductor current is allowed to go negative.
:::

::: exceptional
**An average answer** says "on: inductor charges and current flows from the input; off: inductor discharges to the load" and "the capacitor charges in the on state and discharges in the off state".

**An exceptional answer**:
- Draws two loop diagrams with the current path highlighted and notes that the *input* is not in the loop in the off state.
- States that the inductor current direction never changes in CCM, only its slope.
- Uses KCL to define the capacitor current and gives the zero-crossing times.
- Corrects the common claim that the capacitor tracks the switch state.
- Mentions the exceptions: DCM (zero current) and forced-CCM synchronous operation (negative current).
:::

::: mistakes
- **"The capacitor charges when the FET is on and discharges when it is off."** It switches at the inductor-current crossing of $I_{out}$, which is mid-ramp. In the example the capacitor starts charging 275 ns into the on time.
- **"The inductor current reverses in the off state."** The voltage reverses; the current keeps its direction and just falls.
- **"The diode current flows from ground to the supply."** It flows from ground (anode) up to the switch node (cathode) into the inductor.
- **"The input supplies the load in the off state."** The input is disconnected by the open FET; energy comes from the inductor and the capacitor.
- **Mixing the current direction in the inductor and the capacitor.** The inductor current goes left to right into the output node; the capacitor current is down into the capacitor when charging, up out of it when discharging.
:::

## The capacitor depends on KCL, not the state

At the output node $i_L = i_C + I_{out}$. The load current is nearly constant, so the capacitor current is just the inductor current with its average removed.

## Verify with energy flow

During the on state the source delivers $V_{in}\cdot i_L$; the inductor stores some energy ($\tfrac12L\Delta(i^2)$), and the rest goes to the load and capacitor. During the off state the inductor and the capacitor both feed the load. The input supplies energy *only* while Q1 is on, and the inductor decouples that bursty supply from the continuous load.

## When the directions change

**DCM.** If the load is light, $i_L$ falls to zero during the off state. The diode turns off (it cannot conduct in reverse), $i_L=0$ and the capacitor alone feeds the load until the next on time. In a synchronous buck with a zero-current detector the low-side FET is switched off to avoid this reversal.

**Forced CCM (synchronous).** The low-side FET stays on after $i_L$ reaches zero, so the inductor current goes negative, flowing from the output through the inductor to the switch node and into ground through $Q_2$. The load-side energy is partly returned and then pushed back to the input on the next on time. This improves transient response and constant frequency at the cost of light-load efficiency.

::: applications
- **Choosing which part carries which current**: sizing the diode or low-side FET (average $(1-D)I_{out}$) versus the high-side FET (average $D I_{out}$).
- **Input capacitor sizing**: it carries the input current pulses, as the input is only in the loop during the on state.
- **Efficiency analysis**: conduction loss in each switch depends on the interval in which it carries $i_L$.
- **Reverse-current protection**: ICs with negative-current limits for forced-CCM bucks.
- **Debugging with current probes**: clip on the inductor and expect a triangle; clip on the output capacitor and expect a zero-mean triangle.
:::

::: assumptions
- **CCM, steady state, small ripple.** DCM and forced-CCM variations are noted above.
- **Constant load current** (a processor-like load). With a resistive load the load current has a small ripple, so the capacitor current is $i_L - v_{out}/R$.
- **Ideal switches.** Real parts have dead time during which the body diode carries the current; the direction is unchanged.
- **Conventional current** with arrows from + to −.
- **Positive direction** for the capacitor current is into the positive plate; flipping that convention flips the signs but not the physics.
:::

::: followups
### In the off state, where does the current physically flow?
Around the loop: ground, up through the diode (or low-side FET) to the switch node, through the inductor, into the output node, then through the load (and capacitor) back to ground. The input supply is not part of that loop. See [[q0200]] for who "sources" it.

### Does the inductor current ever reverse in a normal buck?
Not in CCM or standard DCM. In DCM it reaches zero and a diode prevents reversal. In a synchronous buck with forced CCM at light load it does reverse, becoming negative for part of the cycle, which returns energy from the output to the input side and costs efficiency.

### When is the output capacitor charging if the load is a constant 2 A?
Whenever $i_L>2$ A, which is from 275 ns to 1.275 µs in the example: the second half of the on state and the first part of the off state. The rest of the period it is discharging.

### What happens to the capacitor current directions if the load suddenly drops from 2 A to 0.5 A?
The inductor current is still around 2 A and it cannot drop instantly, so for a while $i_C = i_L - 0.5 \approx +1.5$ A: the capacitor charges and $V_{out}$ overshoots until the loop reduces the duty cycle and the inductor current ramps down. Slew is limited to $V_{out}/L=0.70$ A/µs, so about 2 µs to come down by 1.5 A.

### Harder: how does the direction of the current in the input capacitor compare?
The input capacitor current is $i_{HS} - I_{in,avg}$: positive pulses (capacitor supplying the FET, discharging) during the on time and a small negative current (recharging from the source) during the off time. Its average is zero, and the pulses flow *out of* the capacitor into the FET, the opposite direction to the output capacitor's charging. See [[q0193]].
:::

::: interpretations
"On and off states" follow the convention in this section's note: on means the high-side FET conducts and the diode/low-side FET is off. "Direction" is read as conventional current direction in the loop, with the capacitor's charge/discharge direction as a separate sign (into or out of the positive plate). The related questions [[q0190]] and [[q0192]] ask for the timing and for who sinks/supplies the current.
:::

- Input supply is out of the loop in the off state :: Explains the pulsed input current and the need for $C_{in}$.
