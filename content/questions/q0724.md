::: testing
Whether you can tell apart three quantities that all get casually called "the impedance of a transmission line", and give the frequency dependence of each:

1. **Characteristic impedance** $Z_0$: nearly flat at high frequency, but rising at low frequency where $R'$ and $G'$ matter.
2. **Input (looking-in) impedance** of a finite line: depends on length, load and frequency. It repeats with period $v/2\ell$ and swings between $Z_0^2/Z_L$ and $Z_L$. A matched line is flat.
3. **Insertion loss** $|S_{21}|$: the usual meaning of "insertion" in signal integrity, which falls steadily with frequency.

The question wording ("insertion impedance") is unusual, so a good answer states the interpretation first.
:::

::: why
If you assume a trace is "50 Ω at all frequencies" you'll misread a VNA or TDR plot, miss that an unterminated stub looks like a short at its quarter-wave frequency, and misjudge what a source sees. Understanding impedance vs frequency is also how you reason about why matching works and why a mismatch makes frequency-dependent ripples in insertion loss.
:::

::: concepts
- **Characteristic impedance** $Z_0=\sqrt{(R'+j\omega L')/(G'+j\omega C')}$. See [[q0710]], [[q0718]].
- **Input impedance of a line**: $Z_{in}=Z_0\dfrac{Z_L+jZ_0\tan\beta\ell}{Z_0+jZ_L\tan\beta\ell}$.
- **Electrical length** $\theta=\beta\ell=2\pi f t_d$: line length in radians.
- **Standing waves and resonance**: quarter-wave and half-wave behaviours of open and short lines.
- **Insertion loss** $=-20\log_{10}|S_{21}|$, return loss $=-20\log_{10}|S_{11}|$.
- **Matching**: $Z_L=Z_0$ makes $Z_{in}=Z_0$ at every frequency. See [[q0714]].
:::

::: answer
"I'd first say which impedance I mean, since there are three. The characteristic impedance of the line, $Z_0$, is basically flat with frequency: above roughly 10 MHz on a PCB trace it sits at $\sqrt{L'/C'}$, so 50 Ω stays 50 Ω out to many GHz, with only a small drift from loss and dispersion. At low frequency, where the resistance matters, $|Z_0|$ is larger and has a phase, which is the RC regime.

What does vary a lot is the **input impedance** you see looking into a finite piece of line. For a line of electrical length $\theta=2\pi f t_d$ with a load $Z_L$, $Z_{in}=Z_0\frac{Z_L+jZ_0\tan\theta}{Z_0+jZ_L\tan\theta}$. If the line is matched, $Z_{in}=Z_0$ at every frequency. If it isn't, $Z_{in}$ oscillates with frequency, and an open line looks like a capacitor at low frequency, a short at the quarter-wave frequency, an open at the half-wave frequency, and so on. For a 10 cm FR-4 trace that quarter-wave point is around 415 MHz.

If the question means insertion loss, that rises steadily with frequency, roughly $\sqrt f$ from conductor plus $f$ from dielectric, with ripple from reflections if there's a mismatch."
:::

::: technical
**Characteristic impedance vs frequency.** $Z_0(\omega)=\sqrt{(R'+j\omega L')/(G'+j\omega C')}$.
- $\omega L'\gg R'$ and $\omega C'\gg G'$ (above about 1–10 MHz for a PCB trace): $Z_0\approx\sqrt{L'/C'}$, real, nearly constant. Small corrections: $\text{Im}\,Z_0\approx-\frac12\sqrt{L'/C'}(R'/\omega L'-G'/\omega C')$, and slow drift from $\varepsilon_{eff}(f)$ and skin-effect internal inductance.
- $R'\gg\omega L'$ (low frequency, long thin lines): $|Z_0|\approx\sqrt{R'/(\omega C')}$ at $-45^\circ$.

**Input impedance, lossless line of length $\ell$, load $Z_L$:**
$$Z_{in}=Z_0\frac{Z_L+jZ_0\tan\theta}{Z_0+jZ_L\tan\theta},\qquad\theta=\beta\ell=2\pi f t_d$$
- Matched: $Z_{in}=Z_0$.
- Open: $Z_{in}=-jZ_0\cot\theta$. Capacitive for $\theta<\pi/2$, with $Z_{in}\approx1/(j\omega C'\ell)$; zero at $\theta=\pi/2$ ($\ell=\lambda/4$), infinite at $\theta=\pi$.
- Short: $Z_{in}=jZ_0\tan\theta$. Inductive for small $\theta$ with $Z_{in}\approx j\omega L'\ell$, infinite at the quarter-wave.
- Real $Z_L$: $|Z_{in}|$ oscillates between $Z_L$ and $Z_0^2/Z_L$, repeating every $\Delta f=1/(2t_d)$.
- Quarter-wave transformer: $\theta=\pi/2$ gives $Z_{in}=Z_0^2/Z_L$.

**10 cm FR-4 line** ($t_d=0.60$ ns, $Z_0=50$ Ω, $C'\ell=12$ pF, $L'\ell=30$ nH): quarter-wave $f=1/(4t_d)=415$ MHz, half-wave 830 MHz. Open end at 100 MHz: $|Z_{in}|=50\cot(2\pi\cdot10^8\cdot0.60\times10^{-9})=125.7$ Ω, close to the lumped $1/\omega C'\ell=132$ Ω.

**Insertion loss.** For a matched line, $IL(f)=8.686\,\alpha(f)\,\ell$ dB $\approx\ell(a_1\sqrt f+a_2f)$. With mismatches, add a reflection term $-10\log_{10}(1-|\Gamma|^2)$ and ripple with period $1/(2t_d)$ from re-reflections. A 10 cm FR-4 microstrip loses about 0.5 dB at 1 GHz and 2.1 dB at 5 GHz (first-order estimate, see [[q0722]]).

:::

::: long
"The phrase 'insertion impedance' isn't standard, so I'll cover the impedance of the line and the insertion loss.

First, the characteristic impedance. At high frequency it's $\sqrt{L'/C'}$, real and basically constant, so a 50 Ω PCB line stays 50 Ω from tens of MHz to many GHz. There's a small drift: loss gives it a small negative imaginary part, and the effective dielectric constant falls slowly with frequency, so $Z_0$ creeps up a little. At low frequency, below the point where $R'$ equals $\omega L'$, around a MHz for a typical trace, the line becomes RC-like and $|Z_0|$ rises as one over root frequency with a $-45^\circ$ phase.

Second, what you see looking into a finite line. That depends on the load and electrical length: $Z_{in}=Z_0\frac{Z_L+jZ_0\tan\theta}{Z_0+jZ_L\tan\theta}$ with $\theta=2\pi f t_d$. If the load equals $Z_0$ you get $Z_0$ at all frequencies. Otherwise $Z_{in}$ moves around with frequency. An open stub starts out looking like a capacitor, $C'\ell$, then at the quarter-wave frequency it looks like a short, then at half-wave like an open, and repeats. For a 10 cm FR-4 trace the quarter-wave point is about 415 MHz. For a real load, $|Z_{in}|$ oscillates between $Z_L$ and $Z_0^2/Z_L$, period $1/(2t_d)$. That's why a trace with a mismatch shows ripples in a VNA sweep.

Third, insertion loss, which is how much of the signal gets through. It rises with frequency, about $\sqrt f$ from conductor loss and linear from dielectric, so on FR-4 it's about 0.5 dB per 10 cm at 1 GHz and 2 dB at 5 GHz, plus ripple if the line is mismatched."
:::

::: terms
- **Characteristic impedance** $Z_0$: ratio of voltage to current of a single travelling wave.
- **Input impedance** $Z_{in}$: impedance looking into the line terminated by $Z_L$.
- **Electrical length** $\theta$: length in radians, $2\pi f t_d$.
- **Quarter-wave transformer**: a $\lambda/4$ line that maps $Z_L$ to $Z_0^2/Z_L$.
- **Insertion loss**: $-20\log|S_{21}|$, signal lost passing through the line.
- **Return loss**: $-20\log|S_{11}|$.
- **S-parameters**: measured reflection and transmission coefficients vs frequency.
- **Stub**: an unterminated or shorted line segment.
:::

- Writes the input impedance formula and describes open and short stubs: capacitor, short at $\lambda/4$, open at $\lambda/2$.
- Gives a number: 10 cm FR-4 has $\lambda/4$ at about 415 MHz; a 100 Ω load on a 50 Ω line swings $|Z_{in}|$ between 25 and 100 Ω.

:::

::: mistakes
- **Treating $Z_0$ as the impedance you measure at the end of the line.** Only for a matched or infinitely long line. A finite mismatched line's input impedance is a function of frequency.
- **Believing a line's $Z_0$ changes with its length.** It doesn't; $Z_{in}$ does.
- **Saying an open stub is always an open circuit.** It's open only at DC and at half-wave multiples; at quarter-wave it is a short.
- **Ignoring the low-frequency regime.** Below roughly $f=R'/2\pi L'$ the line is not 50 Ω.
- **Mixing up insertion and return loss.** Insertion loss is transmitted loss; return loss is reflected.
- **Reading VNA ripple as noise.** Ripple of period $1/(2t_d)$ in $|S_{21}|$ or $|S_{11}|$ is a standing-wave signature of a mismatch.
:::

::: deep
## Three meanings, one model

Everything starts with the telegrapher's solution: the voltage on the line is a forward wave plus a reflected wave, $V(z)=V^+e^{-\gamma z}+V^-e^{+\gamma z}$, with $V^-/V^+=\Gamma(z)$ set by the load. $Z_0$ is the ratio $V^+/I^+$ for the forward wave alone. $Z_{in}(z)=V(z)/I(z)$ is the ratio of the *total* voltage and current at a point, which includes the reflection. That is why one is a property of the line and the other a property of line plus load.

## $Z_0(f)$: low-frequency, flat, and a slow drift

From [[q0718]]: with $R'(f)=r_0\sqrt{1+f/f_s}$, $L'=301$ nH/m, $C'=120$ pF/m, $\tan\delta=0.02$, the full-model $|Z_0|$ was 1275 Ω at 1 kHz, 403 Ω at 10 kHz, 129 Ω at 100 kHz, 55 Ω at 1 MHz and 50 Ω from about 10 MHz upward, with a phase falling from −44° to under 1°. For digital work above 10 MHz, "$Z_0$ is constant" is a good model.

Two slow effects remain. First, $\varepsilon_{eff}$ drifts down with frequency (dispersion), which raises $\sqrt{L'/C'}$ slightly: for a drop of about 5% in $\varepsilon_{eff}$ the impedance rises about 1–2%. Second, skin effect adds internal inductance that falls with frequency, changing $L'$ slightly. Both are second-order.

## $Z_{in}(f)$ of a finite line

### Why this matters

- A driver sees $Z_{in}(f)$ not $Z_0$ when the load isn't matched: the apparent source load changes with frequency, so switching current and edge shape differ for different harmonics.
- An unterminated stub on a bus presents a low impedance at its quarter-wave frequency, loading the main line there (notches in $S_{21}$).
- A VNA sweep of a mismatched trace shows period-$1/(2t_d)$ ripple in $|S_{11}|$. The ripple spacing gives you the electrical length: $t_d=1/(2\Delta f)$.
- A quarter-wave section transforms impedance: a 70.7 Ω quarter-wave section matches 50 Ω to 100 Ω, at its design frequency only.

## The same thing on a Smith chart

The reflection coefficient looking into the line is $\Gamma_{in}(f)=\Gamma_Le^{-j2\theta}=\Gamma_Le^{-j4\pi f t_d}$. Its magnitude never changes on a lossless line; only its phase rotates, clockwise, at a rate proportional to frequency and line delay. On a Smith chart that is a circle of constant $|\Gamma_L|$ around the centre, and one full revolution takes $\Delta f=1/(2t_d)$ (a half wavelength of line). For $\Gamma_L=1/3$ (100 Ω on 50 Ω) the circle crosses the real axis at $Z=100$ Ω and $Z=25$ Ω, which are the two extremes of the plot above. A longer line makes the circle go around faster, so the ripple in a VNA sweep gets denser. A lossy line spirals inward, since $|\Gamma_{in}|=|\Gamma_L|e^{-2\alpha\ell}$ shrinks with both length and frequency, which is also why a very long lossy cable looks matched at high frequency whatever is on the end: the reflection from the far end dies on the round trip.

This last point is practical: a 30 m coax with 10 dB of one-way loss at 1 GHz presents nearly $Z_0$ at its input even when the far end is open, because the reflected wave returns 20 dB down.

## Insertion loss vs frequency

The transmission through a matched line: $|S_{21}|=e^{-\alpha\ell}$, $IL_{dB}=8.686\,\alpha\ell$. With the first-order FR-4 numbers of [[q0722]], a 10 cm (3.94 in) trace gives about 0.53 dB at 1 GHz and 2.1 dB at 5 GHz, rising smoothly. If the line is mismatched at both ends, multiple reflections add ripple of period $1/(2t_d)$ to $|S_{21}|$ on top of this trend, and the worst-case dips go as deep as $20\log\frac{1-|\Gamma_1\Gamma_2|e^{-2\alpha\ell}}{\ldots}$ relative to the mean. In a real board, vias, connectors and AC-coupling pads add their own frequency-dependent notches.

## Worked example: reading a TDR/VNA

You measure a 10 cm trace on a VNA with the far end open. $|S_{11}|$ is near 0 dB (total reflection) at all frequencies, but the **phase** of $S_{11}$ rotates $2\theta=4\pi ft_d$. The first time the phase rotates a full cycle (from DC to the next return to $0^\circ$) tells you $f=1/(2t_d)$. If that's at 830 MHz, $t_d=0.60$ ns, so with $\varepsilon_{eff}=3.26$ the line is 10 cm long. This is how a VNA gives you length and $\varepsilon_{eff}$, a standard calibration check.

::: applications
- **Stub filters and quarter-wave matching** in RF design: lines used as transformers or notches at a design frequency.
- **VNA and TDR characterization** of cables and PCB traces: extracting length, $\varepsilon_{eff}$, loss and discontinuities from frequency sweeps.
- **Bus stub rules** (DDR, backplane): stubs shorter than $\lambda/10$ of the highest harmonic to avoid notches.
- **Antenna feed lines**: the apparent antenna impedance varies with cable length when mismatched, which is why a mis-tuned antenna reads differently with different coax lengths.
- **Cable loss specs**: dB per 100 ft vs frequency tables for coax selection.
:::

::: assumptions
- **Linear, time-invariant, uniform line**, so one $Z_0$ and one $\gamma$.
- **Lossless line** for the $Z_{in}$ formulas (with small loss added by $e^{-2\alpha\ell}$ softening). Loss reduces the depth of resonances.
- **Single mode (TEM)**; no mode conversion.
- **Constant $\varepsilon_{eff}$** for the electrical length. Dispersion shifts resonances slightly.
- **"Insertion impedance"** interpreted as input impedance or insertion loss. If the interviewer meant $Z_0$ alone, the answer is the first section.
:::

::: followups
### What does a 10 cm open-ended FR-4 stub look like at 100 MHz?
Electrical length $\theta=2\pi\cdot10^8\cdot0.60\times10^{-9}=0.378$ rad (21.7°). $Z_{in}=-j50\cot(0.378)=-j126$ Ω, which is a capacitor of about $1/(\omega\cdot126)=12.6$ pF, near the $C'\ell=12$ pF lumped value.

### At what frequency does a 3 cm open stub on FR-4 look like a short?
Quarter-wave: $f=v/(4\ell)=1.66\times10^8/(4\times0.03)=1.38$ GHz. Below it the stub is capacitive, at it $|Z_{in}|\to0$ and it shorts the line it's attached to; that's why long stubs notch $S_{21}$ in the GHz range.

### How would you use a line section to match a 100 Ω load to a 50 Ω source?
A quarter-wave transformer of impedance $\sqrt{50\times100}=70.7$ Ω. At its design frequency $Z_{in}=Z_0^2/Z_L=70.7^2/100=50$ Ω. The match degrades away from the design frequency, so the bandwidth is limited, and a multi-section transformer widens it.

### Why does a matched line show a flat $Z_{in}$ but a mismatched one doesn't?
With $\Gamma_L=0$ there is only the forward wave, so $V/I=Z_0$ everywhere. With $\Gamma_L\neq0$ the reflected wave adds with a phase $2\beta z$ that changes with position and frequency, so $V/I$ varies.

### Harder: a trace shows deep notches in $S_{21}$ every 1.2 GHz starting at 600 MHz. What is the likely cause?
Periodic notches at $f_n=(2n-1)f_1$ with $f_1=600$ MHz point to a quarter-wave stub resonance (notches at odd multiples of $v/4\ell$). The stub length is $\ell=v/(4f_1)=1.66\times10^8/(4\times6\times10^8)\approx6.9$ mm: a via stub or a branch. If the notches fell at $f_n=nf_1$ the cause would be a half-wave resonance of a mismatched section.

### Scenario: your VNA sweep of a "50 Ω" cable shows 3 dB ripple in $|S_{11}|$ across 1–2 GHz. What do you conclude?
A mismatch somewhere: the ripple's spacing gives the cable's delay (an $f$ spacing of $\Delta f$ means $t_d=1/2\Delta f$), the amplitude gives the reflection coefficient, and since a 3 dB return-loss ripple is a pretty poor match, I'd check connectors, adapters and the termination. A time-gated or TDR measurement locates the reflecting point.
:::

"Insertion impedance" isn't a standard term. Likely intended meanings: (a) the characteristic impedance as a function of frequency (the PCB-version is Q525, [[q0525]]); (b) the **input impedance** looking into the line; (c) the **insertion loss**. I answer all three, starting with the physics they share. The basic loss mechanisms are in [[q0722]], the model in [[q0718]].
